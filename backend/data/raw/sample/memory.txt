On Memory

We do not remember everything, and that is perhaps the point. The mind
keeps what serves it and lets the rest fall away. What survives is not
a recording but a reconstruction, assembled on demand from fragments
that are themselves edited every time they are recalled.

A diary, by contrast, is honest in a way the mind cannot be. The page
keeps what was written, even when the writer would prefer to forget.
To read an old entry is to confront a version of yourself that no
longer exists, and yet whose existence is documented in your own
handwriting. The persistence of the page is what makes the
confrontation possible.

There is a strange continuity here between persons and systems. Both
depend on what they choose to preserve. Both can be replayed, in a
sense, from the artifacts of their past. The library is not the
opposite of the mind; it is its prosthesis.
